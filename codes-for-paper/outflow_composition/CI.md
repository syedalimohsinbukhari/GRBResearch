This is a solid plan — the literature-review pass genuinely earned its keep by catching the r0/Γ_min substitution error before it got coded. A few things worth pushing on before you implement:

**The biggest one: you've already found a better method than the one you're planning to build.** The Li, Gao, Lan & Zhang (2024) precedent — applying Gao & Zhang's closed-form to non-thermal slices via a simulated upper-limit thermal component — is a published, citable, closed-form procedure. Your `zhang_peer_suppression.py` root-find is a from-scratch algebraic translation of a graphical criterion that no paper has ever formalized this way. You flagged this tension yourself in the plan ("worth weighing... before implementation") but the implementation section still specs out the invented root-find as the primary path. I'd resolve this before writing code, not during review afterward: if a referee can ask "why not just use Li et al.'s own method, since you're already leaning on their eq. (8)/Table 2 for validation anyway," you want that answered in the plan, not discovered at revision. If there's a concrete reason the Li et al. approach doesn't fit your episode structure (non-BB episodes without any thermal fit to set an upper limit on, e.g.), say so explicitly — but that reason isn't currently in the doc.

**The r0 anchor has a quiet assumption worth stating out loud.** You're using the pure-fireball r0 (~0.9–1.4×10⁷ cm, from `pe_er_photosphere.csv`) as the trial-value anchor for the Gao & Zhang inversion. But that r0 was derived *under σ0=0*. You're now using it to anchor a calculation whose whole point is to test whether σ0>0. That's not necessarily circular — r0 is usually treated as a central-engine/launch-radius scale that's roughly independent of the outflow's magnetization — but the plan doesn't currently say that reasoning anywhere, and a referee will ask. Worth one sentence in the paper (and in this plan's eventual "Known limitations" section) making the independence assumption explicit. Separately: your anchor is roughly an order of magnitude *below* Gao & Zhang's smallest GRB110721A trial value (10⁸ cm). That's plausible (r0 ranges in the literature do go that low for some progenitor/central-engine scenarios) but it's a large enough departure from the paper's own worked example that it deserves a one-line physical justification or citation, not just "more physically grounded for this burst."

**The fallback logic in `zhang_peer_suppression.py` will make your episodes non-uniform in a way that matters.** R0 = c·δt uses Norris t_v where available, duration otherwise. σ_min depends on R0, so episodes on the t_v branch and episodes on the duration branch aren't really comparable numbers — they're answering slightly different physical questions. That's fine as an engineering fallback, but it needs to be a labeled column (not just an internal implementation detail) so the eventual LaTeX table doesn't present five σ_min values as methodologically uniform when two of them aren't.

**Two things missing from the root-find spec itself**, since you're keeping it (or even if you switch to Li et al.'s method, these still apply to whatever numerical procedure you use):
- No stated check that L_th(σ) is monotonic in σ before you root-find on it. If it isn't monotonic somewhere in the physical range, a naive root-finder can converge to the wrong root or fail silently.
- You handle the σ_min→0 edge case (Verification #3) but not the opposite failure mode: what happens if suppression never brings the prediction below the observed flux even as σ→∞ (within whatever bounded range you search)? That should fail loudly and get reported as "no solution," analogous to how you're already handling the Regime I/IV degeneracy.

**Smaller items:**
- δ=1/3 is fixed to Gao & Zhang's own fiducial choice, which is reasonable, but unlike r0 it gets no sensitivity sweep. If you're going to the trouble of an r0 bracketing sweep to "show sensitivity," a referee may ask why δ didn't get the same treatment, especially since Table 3 (general-δ) exists. Even a one-paragraph "we checked δ=1/3±X doesn't change the regime classification" would preempt that.
- Scope note: GRB080916C-only because it's the only burst with a redshift is a fine reason, but make sure that's stated plainly in the paper's methods (not just this internal plan), since a reader will otherwise wonder why outflow composition doesn't appear for the other three GRBs.
- The Li et al. Table 1 spot-check (validation item 3) is explicitly left incomplete pending F_ob/z extraction — just flagging that this is the one validation step that isn't actually done yet, not just "flagged honestly" as a known gap, in case it quietly slips off the list once implementation starts.

None of this contradicts the plan's decisions — the reasoning on the Γ_min substitution and the regime table is careful and checks out. The main thing I'd resolve before you start coding is the Li et al. method-choice question, since it changes what `zhang_peer_suppression.py` actually needs to do.
